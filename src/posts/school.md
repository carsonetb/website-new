# Dark Times



Across my high school, and many others in America and countries beyond, a dark storm brews, and on the horizon looms a roaring wave. Already, elementary schools are engulfed, and middle schools are struggling for air. It is something that can be felt, in unwillingness. Unwillingness to read and write, to be sincere, to be rational, logical, critical, unwillingness to try. Mental effort, even in the face of an easier path, is rare.

Many make the argument to the older generations that rationality has always been a commodity in youth. Yet, in the past, fringe ideas were most often kept just that. Ideas that offended a portion of the audience had to be filtered out, to appeal to a larger audience. Even if the young people of the twentieth century were just as bad at being critical, they would moderate ideas (at least, to American standards).

This sharp dip in intellectual ability is incredibly worrying, and there are multiple factors causing it. First, and most visible, is social media. Specifically stimulating, short-form content where the viewer has no choice in what they watch. Consumption of this type of content has been shown to consistently decrease willingness to spend mental effort on problems, external or internal. 

Second is economic downturn, which is caused by many factors but made particularly worse by the host of abysmal actions taken by our government. Economic stress tends to further worsen intelligence in a way that compounds with the effects of social media.  

Third is our education system, the last dike holding back rising floodwaters. And rather than reinforcing the barrier, building it up stronger and better, we are slowly destroying it. In the name of saving soil, we are poking holes and springing leaks, at our own peril. The American education system is outdated beyond imagination. It focuses much effort on testing the knowledge of students, while forgetting to test their *thinking* (a much harder, but certainly doable feat to accomplish). But innovation is stagnant in a system filled with bureaucracy, and it seems the only thing the government is willing to do as it pertains to that is to cut funding to the whole system. 

Many people understand the importance of education. But most do not understand that education is, quite literally, something without which our democracy would not exist. Without it, the opinion of the majority would be the whim of an algorithm—or rather, the whim of the people who control it. Critical thinking, intellectual ability, and willingness would be naught. When we cut funding for schools, when we refuse to increase the salaries of educators who have silently upheld our democracy for decades, when we fail to regulate impulsive things like social media and LLMs, we are toying with our freedom. Education is one of the most lucrative investments a country can make, yet we are betting against it. Our losses, as a result, may well be as great or greater as the potential rewards.

We are playing a dangerous game with the young people of our world. In a time where change is becoming ever more vital to the survival of our species, we are crafting a society that will be complacent in the actions of the powerful. Something has to change.
